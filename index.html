02 · About

Hi, I’m Vahri, a product designer who enjoys making complex things feel simple.

I turn complex workflows into clear, thoughtful experiences, balancing how a product works with how it feels. I enjoy finding the structure in a messy problem and shaping it into something that feels natural to use.

I’m curious about the details: what people need, where they get stuck, and how a thoughtful interface can make their day a little easier. For me, good design starts with asking questions and listening closely.

I like the small moments that give a product its personality: clear writing, useful interactions, and motion that helps everything make sense. I want those details to feel considered and purposeful.

This is my little corner of the internet, a place for the work I make, the things I’m exploring, and a bit of play along the way. An open canvas, always taking shape.

“I don’t just design how it works. I design how it moves, how it looks, and how it feels.”
05 · What I believe

What I believe

A few things that guide my work.

Clarity comes first. A useful interface helps people understand where they are, what matters, and what to do next.

Start with people, stay curious. The best questions come from listening to the people living with the problem.

Small details shape the whole. Words, spacing, feedback, and motion all contribute to how an experience feels.

Good design earns trust. Make choices clear, respect people’s attention, and help them feel in control.

Make room to explore. Sketch, try, learn, and refine. The first idea is a starting point, not the finish line.

Play has a purpose. A little personality can make something memorable, as long as it still helps people use it.

If I’ve done my job well, the complexity fades into the background and the experience feels clear, useful, and a little more human.

04 · About the project

From Gmail bill to expense record.

Bills and payment receipts already arrive in Gmail. Kantong is designed to read those emails automatically, pull out the payment details, and turn them into categorized expense records.

The portfolio prototype simulates a Gmail message so you can follow it into Records and see the remaining budget update.

08 · Where there’s room

Let Gmail receipts do the data entry.

Kantong’s core flow starts with a bill or payment receipt in Gmail. It uses the details already in the email, such as the amount and merchant, to create a record. The user can check the source and review where the expense was categorized.

09 · Design principle

Make every Gmail import visible and easy to check.

10 · Design decision
1

Show what the imported expense changed.

Home shows the original allowance beside the amount left. When Kantong records an expense from Gmail, that comparison shows how much of the category budget it used.

11 · Design decision
2

Show when a Gmail receipt has been recorded.

The prototype shows three steps: Email, Read, and Recorded. The final notification confirms the amount and takes the user straight to the new expense in Records.

12 · Design decision
3

Keep a manual path for bills outside Gmail.

When a payment doesn’t come with a Gmail receipt, the user can still enter the amount and category manually. Both paths feed the same expense ledger.

13 · Design decision
4

Trace an expense back to its email.

Records shows where the imported expense came from. Category details connect that expense to the change in the remaining budget.

18 · When it goes wrong

When it goes wrong

A Gmail import can be incomplete, duplicated, or interrupted. Kantong should explain what happened and give the user a way to recover. These are proposed safeguards for the full import flow.

  • Unclear amount. Keep the receipt for review before it affects the budget.
  • Unsure of the category. Let the user choose where the expense belongs.
  • Duplicate receipt. Keep one expense and preserve any correction.
  • Gmail disconnected. Show why imports paused and how to reconnect.
  • No receipt email. Add the payment manually.
